# M2 — IMU measurement and orientation

[Start here](../START_HERE.md) · [Previous: M1](M1.md) · [Next: M3](M3.md)

**Goal:** read the MPU6050, remove static gyro bias, and estimate tilt.

## Measurement model

An accelerometer measures specific force, including the effect of gravity at rest; a gyro measures angular velocity. Angular rate in rad/s multiplied by elapsed seconds gives an angle increment in radians. A gyro offset accumulates into angle drift.

The sensor has six inertial axes, not an absolute heading measurement. Gravity constrains tilt but cannot reveal rotation about the gravity axis. Read the [chip specification](https://invensense.tdk.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/MPU-6000-Datasheet.pdf), its register-map reference, and the [I2C driver guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/i2c.html). Check breakout wiring separately.

## Assignment

1. With power off, connect only the verified IMU supply/ground and chosen SDA/SCL. Confirm pull-up voltage and pin availability first.
2. Initialize a bounded-time I2C transaction, probe the documented address selected by AD0, and check the identity register. Record the actual response; do not treat any responding device as the expected IMU.
3. Configure and read back measurement ranges. Convert signed raw samples using the sensitivity for those exact ranges; store acceleration and gyro in documented units.
4. Record at least 1000 stationary gyro samples after settling. Estimate each bias as the mean; save standard deviation and temperature/conditions. Subtract bias from subsequent gyro samples.
5. Define axes from physical mounting. For an illustrative convention with stationary `az` positive, tilt formulas are `roll = atan2(ay, az)` and `pitch = atan2(-ax, sqrt(ay² + az²))`. Change signs/axis mapping deliberately to match your board.
6. For a small single-axis exercise, combine gyro prediction and accelerometer tilt: `angle = alpha*(angle + gyro*dt) + (1-alpha)*tilt`, with `alpha = tau/(tau+dt)`. Start with `tau=0.5 s`, then measure sensitivity. This scalar form is not a full large-rotation three-axis estimator.
7. Test stationary, known tilt, and return-to-start. Measure drift and bias sensitivity. Translational acceleration disturbs accelerometer tilt; document this limitation.

## Finish when

- Raw and converted samples are logged with timestamps and units; stationary acceleration magnitude is reasonably near 1 g under the chosen calibration.
- Tilt changes in the expected direction and returns near the reference. Propose a ±5° static check initially and report actual error across several poses.
- Disconnecting the IMU gives bounded error handling and an invalid flag, not a locked-up control loop.

## Explain

Why does averaging stationary gyro data help? Why cannot this sensor alone keep absolute yaw correct indefinitely? Why must measured `dt` and the configured sensitivity be used?

**Evidence:** calibration values, orientation plots and limitations; avoid calling estimates ground truth.
